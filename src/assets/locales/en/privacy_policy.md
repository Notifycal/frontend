### Purpose and Privacy Commitment

At **Notifycal** we take privacy and the protection of personal data very seriously. We are committed to safeguarding the privacy of our customers and business partners, which is why we have drafted this Privacy Policy to ensure you have all the information you need about how we collect, use, store and protect personal information in the context of our service.

Our service is built on the trust placed in us by both the professionals and businesses who use our platform, and by their clients who receive reminders. For this reason, we have designed our processes following the principles of privacy by design and by default, minimising data collection to what is strictly necessary to provide the service.

When we collect and process your personal data, we are subject to the **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** (EU) 2016/679, which applies throughout the EEA, and to Spain’s **Organic Law on the Protection of Personal Data and Guarantee of Digital Rights (LOPDGDD)**.

### Important Definitions

To facilitate understanding of this policy, we clarify some terms used throughout this document:

When we say **“User”** or **“Client”**, we mean the professionals, sole traders or businesses who directly contract our services to automate sending reminders to their own clients. They are the ones who hold an active account with Notifycal and configure the reminders.

The term **“Recipient”** or **“End Client”** refers to the individuals who receive the reminders. These are our Users’ clients, and it is important to note that Notifycal has no direct contractual relationship with them.

The **“Service”** encompasses our entire technology platform that enables automated reading of calendar/agenda events and the scheduled sending of reminders.

### Our Role in Data Processing

It is essential to understand that Notifycal operates under two different roles depending on the type of data we process:

We act as the **Controller** when we process the data of our direct Users. This means we determine the purposes and means of processing this information, establishing what we use it for and how we protect it. In this role, we have direct responsibility for these data and are accountable to our Users for their proper processing.

Conversely, we act as a **Processor** with respect to the data of the Recipients of the reminders. In this case, we process the information strictly in accordance with our Users’ instructions, who are the actual Controllers of these data. We do not make decisions about what to do with this information or use it for our own purposes; we simply execute the reminder-sending service as configured by each User.

## 2. Information We Collect

### 2.1. Information About Our Users

When a professional or business decides to use Notifycal, we need to collect certain information to provide the service properly. This collection takes place at different times and for different purposes.

During registration and account creation, we request basic identification data such as the business trade name, the business’s physical address needed for reminders, the company’s sector and size to help tailor the service, and the preferred language to adapt communications. We also collect essential contact information such as the identifier used for sending reminders and the preferred communication channel.

Integration with Google is a key part of our service. When the User authorises the connection, we securely receive and store the access token provided by Google OAuth, which allows us to access calendars on the User’s behalf. We also store the Google account’s unique identifier and the specific permissions granted, which include access to the Google Calendar API and, optionally, to the Google People API. These permissions can be revoked at any time from the User’s Google settings. To revoke access, go to your [Google account](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions), where you will find the list of authorised apps and can remove the permissions granted to Notifycal. If you revoke these permissions, the service will stop functioning until you authorise the connection again.

Use of information obtained through Google APIs complies with the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements, limiting the use of Google data exclusively to provide or improve user-facing features as described in this policy.

During ongoing use of the service, we collect information about how the platform is configured and used. This includes connected calendar configurations and selected message templates, the full history of sent messages so we can provide statistics and resolve potential issues, activity logs that help us detect and prevent improper use of the service, and the dates on which the terms and conditions and privacy policy were accepted.

For payments and billing, we work with a certified payments provider. It is important to emphasise that we do not store any sensitive payment information. We only retain your customer identifier with the provider, the product or plan you are subscribed to, and the balance of credits available for sending messages. All sensitive financial information is managed directly by our payments provider under the highest PCI-DSS security standards, including strong customer authentication measures as required by applicable regulations in each market.

### 2.2. Information About Reminder Recipients

It is crucial to understand that Notifycal does not collect information directly from Recipients. All data we process about them come exclusively from our Users’ calendars or from information that Users manually enter into their events.

The information we process includes the recipient’s contact details when available in the calendar event, and the appointment or event date and time so we can send the reminder at the right moment.

We also process any additional information the User has included in the event and deems relevant to the reminder.

When contact information is not explicitly available, our system identifies the necessary contact information from the event where available. This **optional** feature may use the User’s Google People API to enrich information when needed, accessing only the contact’s phone number and email address.

### 2.3. Technical and Browsing Information

Like most modern web services, we automatically collect certain technical information when our platform is accessed. This includes the IP address used to access the platform, the browser type and version, the device operating system, the pages visited within our platform and the time spent on each, the links followed to reach our site, and various device parameters that help us optimise the experience.

We also use specialised analytics tools to better understand how our platform is used and to continuously improve it. These tools provide aggregated and anonymised metrics about general service usage, as well as user-experience analyses that allow us to optimise the interface without compromising personally identifiable information.

## 4. Cookies Policy and Similar Technologies

### Our Approach to Cookies

At Notifycal we have adopted a minimalist approach to the use of cookies. **We do not use our own (first-party) cookies**, not even to keep the session open (for that we use localStorage). We do not employ cookies for tracking, advertising or marketing. We believe it is possible to offer a quality service without compromising our users’ privacy with unnecessary tracking technologies.

The only cookies you will find on our platform are third-party cookies from our analytics tools, which are activated only if you give your express consent.

### Analytics Technologies

To continuously improve our service, we use two types of tools that help us understand how users interact with our platform:

**Experience analytics tools** allow us to observe anonymous usage behaviours and generate analyses of the most used interface areas. These tools are configured not to capture any information that would personally identify a user.

**Web metrics tools** collect aggregated statistics about platform usage, such as visitor numbers, most-viewed pages, or average session duration. All such information is processed in an aggregated and anonymised manner.

Both tools are activated only if you accept analytics cookies in the consent banner that appears on your first visit.

### Browser Local Storage

In addition to cookies, we use the browser’s local storage to improve your user experience. This technology allows us to temporarily save information on your device such as authentication credentials (to keep the session active), your interface preferences, and unsent form data (to avoid losing information while navigating).

It is important to know that you have full control over these data. You can delete them at any time from your browser settings, although this may require you to sign in again and reconfigure your preferences.

### Consent Management

The first time you access Notifycal, you will see a cookies consent banner that lets you choose exactly which technologies you accept. You can accept all cookies, refuse non-essential ones, or configure your preferences in detail. Your choice will be respected throughout your browsing and you can change it at any time from the privacy settings.

If you decide to refuse analytics cookies, you will still be able to use Notifycal normally. The only difference is that we will not be able to collect anonymous metrics about platform use that help us improve it.

## 6. Information Security

### Technical Security Measures

Data security is one of our top priorities. We have implemented multiple layers of technical protection to safeguard the information you entrust to us:

All communications between your browser and our servers are encrypted using TLS 1.3, the most modern standard for encryption in transit. This means that even if communications were intercepted, their content could not be read.

Authentication on our platform is performed exclusively through secure OAuth systems, which means we do not store passwords in our systems. This removes the risk of passwords being compromised in the event of a security incident.

Our infrastructure is hosted with top-tier cloud service providers in the European Union, using modern architectures that provide inherent security and scalability.

We implement multiple data-protection measures: we perform automatic, periodic backups of all data; credentials and secrets are stored securely with encryption; and we maintain continuous monitoring of our systems to detect anomalous activities or unauthorised access attempts, with automatic alerts that allow us to respond swiftly to any potential incident.

Regarding payment processing, we reiterate that we do not store any sensitive financial data such as card numbers, security codes or banking information. All such information is processed directly by our certified payments provider.

### Organisational Measures

Security is not only a technical matter. We have also established organisational procedures and policies to protect data:

Access to systems and data is strictly limited according to the principle of least privilege. Each team member only has access to the information necessary to perform their specific duties.

All our staff have received specific training in data protection and information security and are subject to confidentiality agreements that continue even after their relationship with the company ends.

We maintain documented incident-response procedures that allow us to act quickly and in a coordinated manner in the event of any security issue. We carry out periodic security audits to identify and remediate potential vulnerabilities before they can be exploited.

### Security Breach Notification

Despite all our security measures, no system is completely infallible. We therefore have a clear protocol in place for the unlikely event of a security breach:

If we detect a breach that may pose a risk to individuals’ rights and freedoms, we will notify the Spanish Data Protection Agency within 72 hours of becoming aware of the incident.

If the breach poses a high risk to your rights and freedoms, we will contact you directly and without undue delay to explain the nature of the incident, the possible consequences, and the measures we have taken or will take to remedy it.

We maintain a documented record of all security incidents, including those that do not require notification, so that we can learn from them and continuously improve our protective measures.

## 8. Data Subjects’ Rights

### Your Rights Under the GDPR

The GDPR recognises a series of rights that you can exercise in respect of your personal data. Whether you are a User of our platform or a Recipient of reminders, you have the following rights:

**Right of access**: You can request confirmation as to whether we are processing your personal data and, where we are, obtain a copy together with information about how we are using them, for what purposes, and with whom we share them.

**Right to rectification**: If you find any of your data are incorrect or incomplete, you may request correction or updating. We want the information we hold about you to be accurate and up-to-date.

**Right to erasure** (also known as the “right to be forgotten”): You may request that we delete your personal data when they are no longer necessary for the purposes for which they were collected, when you withdraw consent, when you object to processing, or when processing is unlawful.

**Right to restriction of processing**: In certain circumstances, you may request that we restrict processing of your data to storage only. This may occur, for example, while data accuracy is being verified or while a claim is being resolved.

**Right to object**: You may object to processing of your data where it is based on our legitimate interests. We will assess your request and cease processing unless we demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms.

**Right to data portability**: You can receive your data in a structured, commonly used format, and you have the right to transmit them directly to another controller where technically feasible.

**Right to withdraw consent**: Where processing is based on your consent, you may withdraw it at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing based on consent before its withdrawal.

## 14. Glossary of Terms

To facilitate understanding of this policy, we include a glossary of the most important technical and legal terms:

**GDPR**: General Data Protection Regulation – Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council, the main European regulation on personal data protection.

**AEPD**: Spanish Data Protection Agency, the independent supervisory authority that ensures compliance with data-protection regulations in Spain.

**DPA**: Data Processing Agreement, a mandatory contract between the controller and the processor under Article 28 of the GDPR.

**SCCs**: Standard Contractual Clauses, a mechanism approved by the European Commission for international data transfers.

**EEA**: European Economic Area, comprising the EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway.

**OAuth**: Open Authorisation, a standard protocol that allows secure authorisation of access to resources without sharing passwords.

**Reminders**: Messages sent through communication channels to notify about upcoming appointments.

**API**: Application Programming Interface, a set of definitions and protocols enabling communication between different software.

**TLS**: Transport Layer Security, a cryptographic protocol that provides secure communications on the internet.

**Token**: A temporary digital credential that authorises access to a specific resource without revealing primary credentials.

**Cookies**: Small text files that websites store on the user’s device for various purposes.

**LocalStorage**: A web-storage mechanism that allows websites to persist data in the user’s browser.
